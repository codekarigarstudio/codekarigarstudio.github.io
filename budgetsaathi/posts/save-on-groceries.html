How to Save Money on Groceries

Groceries are one of the few big expenses you control almost completely. A family spending $800 a month on food can often cut $150–$200 without eating worse — just by shopping smarter. Here are 12 practical tips that actually work.

1. Plan meals, then make a list

Decide the week's dinners before you shop, then write a list and stick to it. Impulse buys are the silent budget killer — shoppers without a list spend significantly more on items they never planned to buy.

2. Never shop hungry

It sounds like a joke, but hunger measurably increases impulse purchases, especially snacks and ready-to-eat food. Eat a snack before you go.

3. Switch to store brands

Store-brand staples — oats, rice, canned tomatoes, frozen vegetables — are often made in the same factories as name brands, at 20–40% lower prices. Try one swap per trip; keep the swaps you can't taste apart.

4. Compare unit prices, not shelf prices

The big pack isn't always cheaper. Check the unit price (per 100g / per oz) on the shelf label — it takes five seconds and regularly exposes "family size" packs that cost more.

5. Buy seasonal produce

Strawberries in winter cost triple and taste like cardboard. Seasonal fruit and vegetables are cheaper, fresher, and better. Frozen produce is picked ripe and is just as nutritious — and never rots in your fridge.

6. Attack food waste first

The cheapest grocery is the one you already bought. Plan one "leftovers night" per week, store food properly (herbs in water, greens with a paper towel), and freeze anything you won't eat in two days. Cutting waste by half can save a family over $1,000 a year.

7. Use cashback apps and loyalty programs

Cashback apps and store loyalty cards turn routine shopping into 2–5% back. Stack them with weekly store ads — buy what's on sale, plan meals around it, not the other way round.

8. Buy staples in bulk — selectively

Rice, pasta, oats, lentils, cooking oil, and frozen vegetables are perfect bulk buys: long shelf life, constant use. But don't bulk-buy perishables or snacks you can't resist — that's not saving, that's pre-paying to overeat.

9. Cut food delivery, batch-cook instead

A $30 delivered meal is $8 of ingredients. Pick two batch-cook recipes (a stew, a tray bake), cook double on Sunday, and freeze half. Even replacing two deliveries a week saves $150+ a month.

10. Shop discount chains and weekly ads

Discount grocery chains consistently undercut big supermarkets on basics. Check weekly ads before planning meals, and don't be loyal to one store — a 10-minute extra stop can save 15%.

11. Grow the easy stuff

Fresh herbs are absurdly marked up. A pot of basil, mint, or chillies on a windowsill costs almost nothing and replaces $4 packets all year.

12. Set a grocery cap and track it

Pick a monthly number (start with last month minus 10%), track spending in a notes app, and review weekly. What gets measured gets managed — most families hit their cap within two months.

Make it a household game

Saving on groceries works best when the whole household plays along. Share your monthly cap with your partner or family, celebrate when you come in under budget, and move half of what you save straight into savings — watching that number grow is the best motivation to keep going. Small, repeated wins beat one heroic month of extreme couponing followed by burnout.
